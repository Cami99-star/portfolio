01The problem

A first period can bring
more questions than answers.

The project materials frame the first-period experience around uncertainty: knowing what to expect, understanding when to change a product, and managing the worry of visible leaks.

Reedy explores how guidance and a companion interface could support those moments. It also considers caregivers who want to help but need clearer ways to explain and prepare.

02Why this project

Design for uncertainty,
with care and restraint.

I designed Reedy as a four-week solo project, moving from desk research and empathy and journey maps to product sketches, an Arduino colour-sensor prototype, and app interface designs.

The audience is framed around young people approaching menarche and their caregivers. Personas and empathy-map statements communicate the design perspective; the materials do not establish a participant sample or clinical findings.

The project reached app interface design and an Arduino colour-sensing prototype. The experiment examines colour response; it does not establish menstrual measurement accuracy or clinical validation.

03The opportunity

Offer a clearer next step,
without adding more anxiety.

The proposed experience connects setup, ongoing guidance, and history. The interface is intended to make the product understandable at the moment of use, rather than expecting someone to interpret the hardware alone.

Because the concept concerns sensitive personal information, the clarity of notifications and the boundaries of what the system can know are part of the design problem.

Design question

How might we help young people feel more prepared for a first period through understandable guidance and a clearer sense of what to do next?

04Design decisions

Three moments.
One connected experience.

The following decisions connect the project’s user needs with its proposed interface and product behavior.

Decision 01 / Start with a clear connection

Use a guided setup
to connect the product.

The proposed onboarding starts in the app and uses a package QR code to identify the product. The flow makes the physical product and the ongoing interface part of the same setup experience.

Setup introduces the link between the product and its companion app.

Decision 02 / Make time visible

Keep ongoing information
and history connected.

The proposed history view groups period dates and durations, while the ongoing view provides the current context. Together, they explore how a person could move between today’s information and past records.

The interface separates current information from a longer-term history.

Decision 03 / Examine the physical signal

Explore sensing
before claiming accuracy.

The Arduino experiment documents a colour sensor, lighting conditions, and code exploration. It is a feasibility step toward investigating the physical concept, rather than evidence that menstrual health can be reliably measured.

The prototype’s colour signals are not clinical measurements. Proposed health labels and predictive reminders require validation before real-world use.

06Reflection

Clarity needs
to come with trust.

For a care-oriented experience, reassuring visuals are not enough. A person needs to understand what a signal means, what the product cannot determine, and what to do when information is uncertain.

What I would test next

Evaluate setup comprehension, interpretation of reminders, and finding relevant history with appropriate participants and safeguards. Examine whether the language feels supportive rather than alarming.

What I would verify next

Investigate sensing feasibility with relevant expertise, and define consent, data privacy, uncertainty, and notification boundaries before treating the concept as a real care product.
