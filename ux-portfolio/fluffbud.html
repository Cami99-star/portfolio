01The problem

A comforting object.
A one-way relationship.

The project starts with people’s attachment to plush toys: familiar objects can become part of routines, travel, and personal expression. FluffBud explores the interaction gap between that attachment and a toy that cannot respond.

I interviewed four users about their relationship with their plush toys. The synthesis highlighted the importance of softness and emotional attachment, interest in a responsive voice, and the practical challenge of taking a companion on trips.

Keep what matters

The physical feeling comes first.

A digital layer should preserve the familiar object and its tactile appeal.

Add an interaction

Personality needs more than a voice.

People should have a way to shape the companion’s appearance and how it responds.

Fit everyday life

The extra hardware must fit in.

The concept needs an accessory that can travel with the toy without dominating it.

02Why this project

Build on the attachment.
Do not replace the plush.

Over six weeks, I independently developed the research, accessory concept, app flows, and visual design. Four user interviews informed the direction, followed by sketches, electronics architecture, preliminary code tests, and app scenarios.

The app supports pairing, device information, visual personalization, and voice settings. Everyday companion scenes are shown as concept storyboards, not evidence of a deployed AI service.

Solo project, designed over six weeks. The work reached app interface design and preliminary Arduino-based hardware testing. The project reflection identifies functional integration and usability evaluation as further work.

↳ Listening to users

Four interviews.
A familiar object worth preserving.

I interviewed four users to understand the relationship they already had with their plush toys. The design opportunity came from those existing habits, rather than assuming that adding AI would be enough.

Insight 01

Softness is part of the value.

The attachment is physical as well as emotional. The accessory should coexist with the plush, preserving its familiar touch.

Insight 02

The toy already has a personality.

Users described familiar companions and individual identities. Customization builds on that relationship instead of replacing it.

Insight 03

Companionship should travel well.

Travel and portability appeared in the interview synthesis. An attachable form makes this a physical-design question too.

Paraphrased themes from the interview synthesis; these are research inputs, not measured outcomes of using the final product.

03The opportunity

Make the interaction
feel like their companion.

The design opportunity is to add interaction while retaining what makes the physical object familiar. Pairing and device status should explain the technology, while customization should leave room for personal expression.

The concept focuses on three moments: connecting the accessory, shaping a companion’s identity, and choosing how its voice behaves.

Design question

How might we add a personal, responsive layer to a familiar plush companion without losing its physical character?

04Design decisions

Three moments.
One connected experience.

The following decisions connect the project’s user needs with its proposed interface and product behavior.

Decision 01 / Make the connection tangible

Pair the accessory,
then show its status.

The proposed pairing flow connects an action on the physical accessory to feedback in the app. A status view makes the connection and device information accessible after setup.

Proposed app screens. The PDF documents preliminary hardware testing; it does not establish a fully integrated pairing and AI-voice system.

Decision 02 / Let personality take shape

Give the companion
a personal identity.

The customization screens let the person explore visual style and voice settings. The intention is to make the companion feel individually chosen, instead of assigning every toy the same interaction.

Visual and voice controls sit within the same customization journey.

Decision 03 / Explore physical feasibility

Design the accessory
and the system around it.

The electronics exploration identifies an ESP32-S3, microphone, audio amplifier, battery-charging module, button, and LEDs. It connects the interaction idea to the components a prototype would need.

Preliminary Arduino-based tests explored the hardware direction. Functional AI integration, product feasibility, and real-user usability testing remained next steps in the project reflection.

06Reflection

The connection should
support the familiar object.

The physical companion is the reason for the experience, not a container for technology. Pairing, accessory size, and personalization all need to work together without obscuring that relationship.

What I would test next

Observe people pairing the accessory, checking its state, choosing a style, and adjusting the voice. Test whether the attachment remains comfortable to carry with the toy.

What I would verify next

Verify microphone and speaker performance, battery life, privacy controls, and the reliability of the voice interaction before making claims about everyday companionship.
